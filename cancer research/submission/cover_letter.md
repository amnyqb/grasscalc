Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit an original manuscript, **"Non-Tobacco Environmental
Carcinogenesis in Exposed Populations: Evidence Grading, Metric Separation, and
a Saudi Eastern Province Registry-Analysis Protocol,"** for consideration in
*Cancer Epidemiology*.

**What this manuscript is.** It is an evidence-mapping review combined with a
pre-specified registry-analysis protocol. It is deliberately *not* presented as
a completed systematic or scoping review: it locks the protocol, eligibility
criteria, full four-database search strategy, charting form, and synthesis rules,
and presents a graded map of curated primary sources, while stating plainly that
the formal multi-database search with populated selection counts is the
registered next step (Section 2.7). I would be grateful if it were assessed in
that category rather than as a completed search.

**Why it fits *Cancer Epidemiology*.** The work sits squarely within the
journal's scope — environmental and occupational cancer risk factors, methodology,
and cancer-registry analysis. It contributes three things the field can reuse:
(1) an explicit A–F evidence hierarchy that keeps legal/policy presumption and
modeled environmental-risk estimates *outside* the internal-validity ladder as
contextual evidence; (2) a metric-separation rule that forbids pooling excess
relative risk per unit dose, observed incidence/mortality ratios, modeled
lifetime risk, and policy-presumptive lists; and (3) a fully specified,
pre-registrable quasi-experimental protocol for testing an environmental-exposure
hypothesis in the Saudi Cancer Registry.

**On the Saudi signal.** The manuscript is careful that the Eastern Province
lung-cancer contrast (3.36 vs 0.59 per 100,000; ≈5.7-fold) is a *crude regional
incidence rate*, not an age-standardized rate, and that the pattern is an
ecological, hypothesis-generating signal at the "Compatible" claim level — not
causal evidence. A transparent smoking-prevalence sensitivity analysis shows that
realistic regional smoking differences cannot, by themselves, reproduce the
observed contrast.

**Declarations.** This manuscript is original, has not been published previously,
and is not under consideration elsewhere. There are no conflicts of interest and
no external funding. As a sole-authored evidence map and protocol with no new
human-subjects data, ethics approval was not required for this article; it will
be obtained for the proposed Saudi Cancer Registry microdata analysis. The
charting/extraction sheet, search strategy, reference register, and
smoking-sensitivity computation accompany the submission and will be deposited on
the Open Science Framework.

Thank you for considering this work. I would be glad to suggest reviewers with
expertise in radiation epidemiology, environmental carcinogenesis, or
cancer-registry methodology on request.
